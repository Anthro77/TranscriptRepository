Here's how I break it down:
-- _Analytical_ statements are statements about the apperance I.E objective reality as mediated by the senses. To make an analytical statement, you take in the raw data of things in themselves and you _analyse_ them to draw some kind of observation that is true to the extent that your senses haven't fooled you. So if you can see that a shape is a square, you can say, "That's a square" as an analytical statement. That statement can only be wrong if your perception of the square is wrong. But if it's correct (say we've corroborated it with other witnesses) then it can't be false unless shown otherwise. Because, at the end of the day, your perception of the square isn't the _ding en sich._
. . . Or at least this is how analytical statements _would_ work if it weren't the case that the cocneptual rules that define squares at shapes weren't independent of observation. I.E a square will always have four sides by definition. So even if you were wrong and the object wasn't a square, you still assumed it was a square because of what you know to be the definition of a square. This is where we get the analogy that analytical statements are really just logic. It's a filter that is consistent now matter what circumstance passes through it. You can always use logic to try and break something down (whether or not it's actually productive to do so.)
-- _Synthetic_ statements are statements that are drawn from analystical statements. To make a synthetic statement, you _synthesize_ what you've learned from analytical statements to make a further observation.
