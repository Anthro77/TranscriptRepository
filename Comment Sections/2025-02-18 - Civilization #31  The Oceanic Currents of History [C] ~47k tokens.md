The Greco-Roman thinker who most clearly explained the transition from an oligarchy to a democracy through the oligarchy's own corruption, as part of a repetitive political cycle, is Polybius (c. 200 – c. 118 BCE).
Polybius's Anacyclosis
Polybius, a Greek historian deported to Rome, developed a theory called Anacyclosis (or constitutional cycle), which describes the inevitable and cyclical succession of six forms of government.
According to his model, the transition from an oligarchy to a democracy unfolds as follows:
 * Oligarchy Degenerates: The aristocracy (rule by the best) becomes corrupt and transforms into an oligarchy (rule by the wealthy and powerful few). The oligarchs become greedy and oppressive, using their wealth to seize the people's property and abuse their wives and children.
 * Popular Revolt: The people, outraged by this injustice and economic tyranny exercised by the oligarchs, rise up in revolt.
 * Establishment of Democracy: The people seize power and establish democracy, a system where liberty and equal rights are proclaimed.
This cycle continues because Polybius predicts that this democracy will, in turn, become corrupt and devolve into Ochlocracy (mob rule or the "tyranny of the majority"), before a new strongman establishes a monarchy, thus restarting the loop.
