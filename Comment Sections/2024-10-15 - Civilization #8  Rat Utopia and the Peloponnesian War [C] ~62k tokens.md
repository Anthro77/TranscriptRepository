> * Culture and Philosophy: The central cultural belief was Eudaimonia, a concept translated as "human flourishing." This was the ideal of being the best one can be. The quintessential example is Achilles from Homer's Iliad, who chose to "die young as a hero" rather than "live a long time as a nobody." This mentality created an intensely competitive, even backstabbing, society where individuals sought glory.

A citizen deemed too competitive or a threat to the social order could be banished by popular vote for ten years. >   * Consolidating Power: Pericles, representing the "lower nobility," championed democratic reforms that gave every citizen the right to vote. >   * "Official Corruption": Pericles moved the Delian League's treasury from Delos to Athens. He then used this immense wealth to fund massive public works projects, most famously the Parthenon. This spending provided jobs and enriched his supporters, functioning as a system of official bribery to maintain popular support.  
>   * Building an Empire: When members of the Delian League tried to leave, angered by the misappropriation of their funds, Pericles used military force to compel them to stay.
